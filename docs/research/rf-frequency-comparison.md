# Rocket Avionics RF frequency comparison (Taiwan)

Date: 2026-08-25. Scope: a Main 920-925 MHz E22 path and a frequency-diverse Backup path carrying the **same complete binary v3 snapshot**. This is research, not an RF selection, certification result, flight approval, or range guarantee.

## Evidence boundary

### Verified

- The repository deliberately leaves Backup RF unselected: it must differ in frequency from Main's 920-925 MHz E22 path and pass NCC applicability, complete-v3 airtime, and a measured link budget before freezing. The v3 frame size and rate are also intentionally open until the weaker measured path is known. [ADR-0021](../adr/0021-replace-the-unverified-433-backup-rf-assumption.md), [ADR-0008](../adr/0008-size-v3-for-the-weaker-rf-link.md)
- Taiwan's current low-power baseline is NCC **LP0002**, revised 2024-02-06. Its required warning says low-power RF equipment must not affect flight safety or interfere with lawful communications; interference requires immediate cessation until corrected, and the equipment must tolerate lawful/ISM interference. LP0002 has no rocket-specific operating or altitude authorization. [NCC LP0002](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278)
- A foreign module/IC data sheet or FCC/CE document is not evidence that the final rocket radio, its antenna, firmware parameters, and installation satisfy LP0002. In particular, LP0002's high-power paths impose waveform, bandwidth, PSD, antenna, and out-of-band limits. [NCC LP0002](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278)

### Engineering inferences (must be measured)

- Frequency diversity reduces a common-band interference failure, but cannot establish independent reception: the two flight antennas can still share obstruction, polarization nulls, placement, power noise, launch-site interference, or a ground-station failure.
- Every comparison below assumes the final airborne and ground antennas, coax loss, radiation pattern while rolling, launch geometry, regulatory power limit, receiver blocking, and packet error rate are separately measured. A component sensitivity or a free-space calculation is not a range claim.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

FH/digital systems have their own requirements. [NCC LP0002 §5.8](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | 2400-2483.5 MHz permits FH or digital modulation. Digital modulation is <=1 W conducted, but must have >=500 kHz 6-dB bandwidth and <=8 dBm in any 3 kHz; FH has channel/dwell requirements. [NCC LP0002 §4.10](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | 2.4 GHz LoRa is **not automatically compliant** merely because SX1280 supports it. A narrow setting below the 500 kHz measurement criterion cannot use the digital-modulation path without a different applicable classification and final-device test. |
| Representative radio data | The project names only “E22”, not a part number. For the representative E22-900T22S, the maker states 850-930 MHz, +22 dBm, -131 dBm at 2.4 kbps/SF11, and 2.4-62.5 kbps air rate. [E22-T user manual](https://www.ebyte.com/downpdf/1587.html) | SX1280 supports 2400-2500 MHz, LoRa/FLRC/(G)FSK, +12.5 dBm, and sensitivity down to -132 dBm. Semtech lists LoRa SF5-12 and 200-1600 kHz bandwidth; its product guide lists LoRa from 476 bps to 250 kbps. | Data-sheet-only receive headroom | The representative E22 numbers imply +22 - (-131) = **153 dB** under the maker's stated setting. | Semtech's advertised extreme figures imply +12.5 - (-132) = **144.5 dB**; the two extrema are not the same PHY setting. [sources above](#direct-920-925-mhz-e22-versus-24-ghz-sx1280) | The 8.5 dB numerical difference is an **inference**, not a link budget or a range ratio. A 30 dBm E22 variant would produce a different result. |
| Free-space frequency term | At 920 MHz, the lower carrier has less free-space loss. | At the same range with equal antenna gains, 2.4 GHz has `20 log10(2400/920) = 8.3 dB` more free-space loss. [ITU-R P.525-5](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.525-5-202411-I/en) | Combining the representative component figures with that 8.3 dB gives roughly **16.8 dB less unqualified margin** for SX1280/2.4 GHz. This is a comparison aid only; it must not be translated into distance. |
| Antenna scale and integration | A quarter-wave at 922.5 MHz is about 81 mm. | A quarter-wave at 2.4 GHz is about 31 mm. These are calculations from wavelength/frequency, not antenna designs. [ITU-R P.525-5](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-P.525-5-202411-I/en) | 2.4 GHz can be physically compact; 920 MHz gives a larger practical radiating structure. Neither result proves radiation pattern or efficiency inside the airframe. |
| Airframe / antenna risk | Ebyte says antenna structure materially affects performance; it recommends an exposed antenna and warns that a metal enclosure or nearby metal severely attenuates the signal. [E22-T user manual](https://www.ebyte.com/downpdf/1587.html) | The same installation risks need a separate SX1280-on-final-airframe measurement; no loss number is assumed from the E22 manual. | The validation article must include installed S11/efficiency or a substitute antenna test, azimuth/elevation pattern, roll/polarization sweep, and powered EMI test for **each** band. |
| Coexistence | LP0002 supplies no exclusive channel guarantee and requires tolerance of lawful/ISM interference. [NCC LP0002 §3.8](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | Bluetooth alone occupies 2400-2483.5 MHz; coexistence with Wi-Fi and other 2.4-GHz equipment is therefore an actual design condition. [Bluetooth Core radio spec](https://www.bluetooth.com/wp-content/uploads/Files/Specification/HTML/Core_v6.3/out/en/br-edr-controller/radio-physical-layer-specification.html) Semtech says SX1280 Wi-Fi immunity varies with SF, bandwidth, Wi-Fi type, and frequency offset, and recommends avoiding occupied portions of the band. [Semtech FAQ](https://www.semtech.com/design-support/faq/P60) | Do a launch-site spectrum survey and injected-interferer PER test. Do not assert that either band is “cleaner” without measurements at the launch site and time. |
| Directional ground antennas | For 920-925 MHz, a transmitting directional antenna over 6 dBi requires matching conducted-power reduction. [NCC LP0002 §5.8](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | The analogous 2.4-GHz non-fixed-point-to-point rule also reduces power above 6 dBi. [NCC LP0002 §4.10](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | High gain is normally most useful on the **ground receiver** for downlink; a ground transmitter for command must include the applicable conducted-power reduction in its EIRP budget. A moving rocket is not “fixed point-to-point.” |

## Airtime and throughput: what can and cannot be concluded now

- The v3 snapshot has no frozen byte length or transmission rate, by design. Therefore no candidate has a verified complete-v3 airtime yet. [ADR-0008](../adr/0008-size-v3-for-the-weaker-rf-link.md)
- At a stated PHY rate `R`, even an ideal `N`-byte payload has a lower bound of `8N/R` seconds before preamble, coding, headers, CRC, inter-packet timing, retries, or listen-before-talk. Thus a 64-byte *example* lower bound is 213 ms at 2.4 kbps, 8.2 ms at 62.5 kbps, and 1.08 s at 476 bps. The actual ToA must be obtained from the locked PHY/configuration and then tested. [E22-T user manual](https://www.ebyte.com/downpdf/1587.html), [Semtech product guide](https://www.semtech.com/uploads/design-support/SEMTECH_LORA_PG.pdf)
- Faster configurations reduce airtime/latency but do not by themselves improve the link: Ebyte explicitly says higher air rate shortens latency while reducing sensitivity/range. [E22-T user manual](https://www.ebyte.com/downpdf/1587.html)
- For any fixed snapshot period, calculate `airtime / period` separately for Main and Backup, include both only if they are simultaneously active, and test the receiver under intended interference. Do not satisfy the common-frame requirement by reducing Backup fields. [ADR-0007](../adr/0007-include-raw-sensor-data-in-every-v3-snapshot.md)

## Other material frequency / transport routes

| Route | Verified regulatory or service facts | Fit for continuous complete telemetry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **5.725-5.850 GHz U-NII / Wi-Fi-class custom link** | LP0002 allows <=1 W conducted and <=30 dBm/500 kHz PSD; bandwidth must be >=500 kHz. Non-fixed-point-to-point use above 6 dBi has conducted-power reduction. [NCC LP0002 §5.7](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | Technically a high-throughput different-band approach, but compared with 920 MHz has `20 log10(5800/920) = 16.0 dB` more free-space loss at equal distance/gains. The high-gain exemption is for fixed point-to-point, so it does not describe the rocket link. Require tracking/pattern and interference testing. |
| **5.25-5.35 or 5.47-5.725 GHz U-NII** | These ranges require TPC and DFS/radar detection; initial channel availability check is 60 s, radar detection triggers channel move/stop rules, and a radar channel has a 30-minute non-occupancy period. [NCC LP0002 §5.7.7](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | A poor deterministic flight-telemetry candidate unless its required dynamic channel behavior is explicitly acceptable and verified. |
| **433-435 MHz LoRa-style fallback** | LP0002's intermittent/periodic class forbids continuous data for control use and restricts other transmission to <1 s with >10 s and >=30x-on-time rest. [NCC LP0002 §4.4](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | Not a normal continuous full-telemetry allocation. It must not be treated as an approved default merely because 433-MHz modules exist. |
| **429 / 449 MHz low-power radio data transmitter** | LP0002 specifically defines six 429-MHz frequencies and ten paired 429/449-MHz channels for data transmitters: <=10 mW ERP, <=8.5 kHz bandwidth, specified F1/F2 emissions, and burst/rest limits. [NCC LP0002 §5.3.3](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | A legally named narrowband-data path, but it needs a different modem and a new link/throughput design; it is not a drop-in LoRa alternative. |
| **467-MHz FRS** | FRS permits <=1 W ERP but non-voice data is limited to person-to-person/text/SOS/location use; manually initiated digital messages are <=1 s and <=one per 30 s, with no store-and-forward. [NCC LP0002 §5.5](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | Not general live flight telemetry. |
| **72-MHz model-aircraft control** | LP0002 permits 72.00-72.99 MHz model-aircraft remote control at <=0.75 W ERP and <=8 kHz, but restricts it to one-way control. [NCC LP0002 §5.3.1](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278) | Not the required bidirectional/full-binary telemetry service. |
| **Amateur bands (including 430 MHz / 2.4 GHz)** | Amateur operation may not use unapproved cipher/code communication, and may not install an amateur station on an unmanned aerial vehicle; data modes remain subject to the amateur table and monitoring provisions. [NCC Amateur Radio Rules §§38, 41](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094239) | Exclude as the default rocket telemetry route; this report makes no claim that a rocket is outside the UAV/similar-device restriction. |
| **NCC/MODA assigned experimental frequency** | An experimental R&D private telecom network uses a MODA-assigned frequency. Network and stations require approval, inspection, and licensing unless the equipment fits the listed low-power approval path; station applications identify frequency, bandwidth, power, quantity, model, and technical specs. [Experimental R&D private-network rules §§2-3, 21-23](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/reFormatFLAWDAT08.aspx?id=FL094215) | The path for a bespoke licensed channel/power plan, not an immediately usable band. It needs early regulator and launch/airspace coordination. |
| **Public cellular LTE/5G/NB-IoT** | NCC's terminal specification covers carrier-managed 700/900/1800/2100/2500/2600/3500/28000 MHz bands, depending on technology. [NCC mobile-terminal spec](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/reFormatFLAWDAT08.aspx?id=FL095122) | A service/coverage architecture rather than a controllable single RF channel. It can be evaluated as an auxiliary transport only after operator, altitude, latency, and coverage testing; no deterministic flight-link claim is made here. |
| **L-band satellite messaging** | Iridium operates 1616-1626.5 MHz. An official Iridium SBD product states messages up to 340 bytes; this is a service-mediated path, not a local unlicensed link. [Iridium frequency](https://www.iridium.com/products/satcom-antenna-iridium/), [Iridium SBD product](https://www.iridium.com/products/rock-seven-rockblock-plus/) | Potential independent long-distance/recovery transport, but not a replacement for continuous low-latency telemetry without an end-to-end service, antenna-dynamics, cost, and current Taiwan terminal-approval assessment. ## Validation gates before selecting any Backup frequency

1. Freeze exact Main E22 part number, radio firmware, centre frequency, output power, PHY bandwidth/SF/CR, and final antenna; independently lock the SX1280 candidate's equivalent configuration.
2. Obtain an NCC applicability review for the **final configuration**: 2.4-GHz LoRa must specifically demonstrate its >=500-kHz 6-dB bandwidth digital-modulation path (or another valid classification), PSD, band edge/spurious emissions, antenna gain, and required device-approval route. [NCC LP0002](https://ncclaw.ncc.gov.tw/FLAW/FLAWDAT0202.aspx?id=FL094278)
3. Measure installed antenna matching/efficiency and 3-D pattern with representative battery, wiring, enclosure, and both radios powered; repeat through expected temperature/vibration and roll/polarization conditions.
4. For the frozen complete-v3 frame, measure ToA, packet error rate, receiver blocking, RSSI/SNR margin, and ground-receiver behavior at the planned rate over a geometry and launch-site interference profile that covers ascent, apogee, descent, and recovery.
5. Verify command-link behavior separately from telemetry and retain the existing prohibition on in-flight unsafe authority changes. [CONTEXT](../../CONTEXT.md)

## Source quality note

All external sources above are the regulator, radio manufacturer, standards owner, or satellite-service owner. “Range”, “margin”, and candidate suitability statements are explicitly marked as inference unless they quote a source's stated device limit.
